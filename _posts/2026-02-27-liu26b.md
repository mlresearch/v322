abstract: Knowledge distillation (KD) is a widely adopted technique for compressing
  large models into smaller, more efficient student models that can be deployed on
  devices with limited computational resources. Among various KD methods, Relational
  Knowledge Distillation (RKD) improves student performance by aligning relational
  structures in the feature space, such as pairwise distances and angles. In this
  work, we propose Quantum Relational Knowledge Distillation (QRKD), which extends
  RKD by incorporating quantum relational information. Specifically, we map classical
  features into a Hilbert space, interpret them as quantum states, and compute quantum
  kernel values to capture richer inter-sample relationships. These quantum-informed
  relations are then used to guide the distillation process. We evaluate QRKD on both
  vision and language tasks, including CNNs on MNIST and CIFAR-10, and GPT-2 on WikiText-2,
  Penn Treebank, and IMDB. Across all benchmarks, QRKD consistently improves student
  model performance compared to classical RKD. Importantly, both teacher and student
  models remain classical and deployable on standard hardware, with quantum computation
  required only during training. This work presents the first demonstration of quantum-enhanced
  knowledge distillation in a fully classical deployment setting.
